Lokalola is an integrated, community-based organic waste processing system that converts local organic waste into renewable energy and useful products, helping communities process their waste independently.

Waste protocol

Three lanes, one simple rule: sort at home.

  • Wet Food waste 35 kg/day, blue bin Biodigester Fermented without oxygen into biogas and digestate.
  • Dry Leaves, twigs etc. 15 kg/day, brown bin Pyrolysis Heated without oxygen into biochar.
  • Not organic Plastics & others Not processed by the unit Rejected Kept out so it never disturbs the process.

How it works

From kitchen waste to fertiliser, with heat in the middle.

Waste is the source, biogas is the stored energy, and heat that replaces LPG is the measured use. Explore the unit part by part.

Select a number (or use the arrow keys) to see each part.

1

Biodigester

3 × 2,000 L HDPE tanks in series · 4 m³ working volume · 52-day retention · anaerobic

Wet food waste (35 kg/day) ferments without oxygen and releases biogas. The digestate is then pasteurised at 70 °C so it is safe to use in fertiliser.

2

Biogas store

3.5–4.7 m³/day · H₂S scrubber · ~2.5 m³ gas bag

Biogas is cleaned by an H₂S scrubber, then stored in a gas bag. This is the stored energy: it heats the pyrolysis retort and delivers 76–101 MJ of heat a day, about 200 LPG cylinders (3 kg) a year.

3

Dryer & dry-waste inlet

15 kg/day of leaves and twigs

Dry waste from the brown bin goes in here, is dried, then fed to pyrolysis. Plastics and other non-organics are rejected.

4

Pyrolysis retort

1 retort (~110 L) · 300–400 °C · heated by biogas

Dry waste is heated without oxygen using the heat from the biogas and turns into biochar, a stable carbon that is later enriched into fertiliser.

5

Fertiliser tank

~17 kg solid + ~60 L liquid per day

Biochar is enriched with pasteurised digestate into a clear, ready-to-sell fertiliser for nearby farmers.

6

IoT control panel

ESP32-S3 · 4 type-K thermocouples · pH · gas flow meter · MQTT/4G · microSD log

One START button. Temperature, timer and safety are automatic and data reaches the server by itself: smart on the inside, so the operator's job stays simple.

Energy & carbon impact

From waste to stored biogas to measurable heat

Lokalola does not sell energy as its main product. But every package carries a complete energy chain: a source, a store and a measured use that replaces a fossil heat source, LPG.

Energy chain per 50 kg package

  1. 1 · Source

    Food waste, digested

    35 kg of food waste a day is digested without oxygen in the biodigester.

  2. 2 · Storage

    Biogas, 2.5 m³ in a bag

    Biogas is cleaned by an H₂S scrubber, then stored in a gas bag: about 2.5 m³ on hand.

  3. 3 · Measure

    Every number logged

    A biogas flow meter and 4 thermocouples feed automatic IoT logging, so the energy is measured, not assumed.

  4. 4 · Use

    Heat that replaces LPG

    Heat for pyrolysis at 300–400 °C. Waste heat pasteurises the fertiliser and dries the feedstock.

Carbon impact per package, per year

One 50 kg package avoids or stores

0t CO₂e / year

Three parts add up: 7.0 + 2.7 + 1.8. Open each bar to see how we calculated it.

011.5 t CO₂e

Landfill methane avoided 7.0 t CO₂e How we calculated it

One package digests about 35 kg of food waste a day, roughly 12.8 tonnes a year. Left in a landfill, that waste would rot without oxygen and release methane. Using the US EPA WARM v16 factor of 0.50 t CO₂e per short ton, avoiding it is worth about 7.0 t CO₂e a year.

12.8 t food waste × 0.50 t CO₂e per short ton (EPA WARM v16)

Stored in biochar 2.7 t CO₂e How we calculated it

Dry leaves and twigs are heated in the pyrolysis retort and become about 1.8 t of biochar a year. Biochar is about 65% carbon, 3.67 converts carbon to CO₂, and only about 65% of it stays stable in soil for the long term (IPCC 2019 permanence factor). The result is calculated from unrounded inputs.

1.8 t biochar × 65% C × 3.67 × 65% stable (IPCC 2019)

LPG replaced 1.8 t CO₂e How we calculated it

The biogas gives about 76 MJ of heat a day. LPG holds about 46 MJ per kg, so that is roughly 1.65 kg of LPG a day that no longer has to be burned. Each kg of LPG releases 2.98 kg of CO₂.

76 MJ/day ÷ 46 MJ/kg × 2.98 kg CO₂/kg × 365 days

Assumptions behind these numbers
  • Coverage: one 50 kg package serves about 150 households, so packages = households ÷ 150, rounded up. Each unit needs roughly 8 × 5 m of land.
  • Per package, per day: 50 kg of waste (35 kg food + 15 kg dry), 3.5–4.7 m³ of biogas, 76–101 MJ of heat, and about 17 kg solid + 60 L liquid fertiliser.
  • Per package, per year: about 200 LPG cylinders (3 kg) replaced and 11.5 t CO₂e avoided or stored.
  • Money: unit price IDR 85 M. Service Basic IDR 0.5–1 M per month per unit. Owner benefit of +IDR 2.0–2.65 M per month net per unit: hauling cost avoided, fertiliser sales and LPG replaced, minus electricity and full service.
  • Scaling: every result scales in a straight line with the number of packages. Ranges come from the design range of the source figure. This is an estimate, not a forecast or an offer.

Strategy insight

We sell the certain thing: the facility.

Reselling biochar and biogas is uncertain, so we don't bet on it. The buyer gets a real facility, and afterwards only spare parts and repairs.

  • A clear output. The unit makes fertiliser automatically, so owners get a ready-to-sell product instead of abstract raw biochar that could invite competition.

  • Smart inside, simple outside. IoT handles temperature, timer and safety. The operator just presses one START button.
