Waste is energy in the wrong place.

Lokalola plans to put it back in the right one: a hub we own and run in Yogyakarta, turning organic waste from hotels and restaurants into biochar and fertilizer, with a monthly processing certificate for each client. A 50 kg per day pilot comes first, then a 750 kg per day hub.

02 / 13 · Problem

Almost 40 percent of Indonesia’sreported waste is food waste.

The National Waste Management Information System (SIPSN) of the Ministry of Environment records food waste as the largest type of waste in 2024: 39.87 percent of the total, based on reports from 303 regencies and cities.

Yogyakarta is moving from landfill to treatment at source. Since 1 January 2026, city depots accept only residue, and Sleman has pushed organic treatment at source since 2024. Hotels and restaurants need somewhere to process organic waste, with proof.

Every hotel and restaurant produces organic waste in the same spot, every day.

Lokalola plans to process it for them and prove it every month.

03 / 13 · Model

Three income lines,one hub

Lokalola owns and operates the hub. Clients need no capital spending, no land, and no operator. All prices below are planned, with no signed clients yet.

Hotels, restaurants, cafés

Paid processing per kg

Planned fees: Rp1,000/kg for hotels and Rp600/kg for restaurants and cafés, billed monthly. Each client gets a monthly processing certificate from Lokalola, based on IoT weight and process data.

Nurseries, landscapers, urban farmers

Biochar, liquid fertilizer, compost

Planned prices: biochar Rp5,000 to 12,000/kg, liquid fertilizer Rp5,000 to 15,000/L, compost Rp1,300/kg. Product quality has not been lab-tested.

CSR partners and sub-districts

Programs and training

Implementation fees from CSR programs, community training, and education visits at the hub. Nothing has been contracted.

Dry-waste suppliers are not paid and do not pay; they receive 20% of the biochar. Biogas only heats our own retort, so no energy is sold. Biochar is the char the retort produces. Projected revenue mix in year 5: 20% processing, 48% products, 31% programs.

Every kilo processed,proven every month

Hotels and restaurants now have to finish their organic waste, and hauling alone does not show where it went. Our clients are planned to receive a monthly processing certificate built from weighed, logged data.

  • For guests and owners

    A number the business can show: kilos diverted and estimated emissions avoided.

05 / 13 · How it works

From food waste to char,in one system

Food waste and dry biomass go in. Biochar, liquid fertilizer, and compost come out. Biogas from the digester heats the retort, so no energy is sold.

Terms
  • Digester: a closed tank where bacteria break food waste down into biogas.
  • Biogas: a flammable gas (mostly methane) made when material rots without air.
  • Retort: a kiln built as a closed tube that heats dry chips without air until they become char.
  • Digestate: the liquid and solids left over from the digester. Its quality as fertilizer has not been tested.
  • Char (also called biochar): what is left when chips are heated without air. Its quality has not been tested.
  • Dry chips: dry biomass cut to 20 mm or smaller (design assumption), from dry-waste suppliers.

Digester

Gives a rough sense of the size through your phone camera. It is not a measuring tool. Equipment footprint 10.1 × 6.5 m (design).

Open this page on a phone to try AR.

  • Digester. A 5,000 L HDPE tank, outdoors. Food waste becomes biogas here.
  • Gas module. A 2.5 m³ bag, a sulfur filter, and a pressure relief, under a netted canopy for natural ventilation. The net opening has not been tested.
  • Feed station. Sorting table, mixing tank, and pump. Food waste enters here every day.
  • Retort. A stainless steel tube with a screw conveyor. Dry chips move slowly, are heated without air, and leave as char.
  • Chimney. 4.34 m tall (design). Combustion exhaust leaves here, above the operator’s head.
  • Char bin. A closed bin with a water seal. Char is removed once cool, below 80 °C.
  • Operator panel. Start and stop buttons, green, orange, and red status lights, and an emergency stop. Safety does not depend on the screen.

Is the machine safe?

We are designing it to meet EN 298 for burner control and the Permenaker 5/2018 exposure limits for hydrogen sulfide. The design keeps flames at least 5 m from gas equipment, installs it in open air, and uses flame arrestors, methane detection, and hard-wired emergency stops. An independent hazard review comes before the first run.

B1 milestone
  1. Q1

    Can the retort run on its own heat?

    At 5 kg of chips per hour, the main flame is turned down to a small flame, and the tube wall stays above 420 °C for at least two hours.

  2. Q2

    How much gas does a cold start take?

    Target: 30 MJ or less, about 1.5 m³ of biogas. Current calculation: 32–38 MJ, so this target is a stretch.

  3. Q3

    Is tar under control?

    After five sessions of 30 kg each, the vapor holes are still at least 80 percent open, the motor does not jam, and no tar clumps in the hopper.

  4. Q4

    Is the char good quality?

    Tested in a laboratory. Target: volatile matter 25 percent or less, and fixed carbon 65 percent or more.

Summary; the full criteria are in the B1 test plan.

Backup plan, not a promise

If the results are poor: if Q1 narrowly fails, we raise the feed rate or add heat retention. If it fails by a wide margin, we fall back to a drum design, which would need about 45 kg of food waste per day instead of 35. That would raise the supply we need to secure.
