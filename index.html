Managed locally, for greater energy!

Waste is energy in the wrong place.

Lokalola is designed to put it back in the right one: a facility we plan to run ourselves at the edge of a city, turning food waste from food businesses and dry biomass from agribusinesses into biogas and char. First pilot target: 35 kg food waste per operating day and 15 kg dry chips per day on average.

03 / 11 · Model

Two kinds of waste,one facility

Lokalola plans to run the facility itself, at the edge of a city, between food businesses and agribusinesses. Our team operates the equipment. Suppliers hand over the waste.

Food and drink businesses

A paid processing service

You hand over wet organic waste, and Lokalola processes it. This is the main income. Pricing has not been set.

Agribusinesses

One third of the biochar

You hand over dry organic waste. One third of all the biochar produced goes back to agribusiness suppliers. How it is divided among suppliers, and whether the service carries a fee, has not been set.

Lokalola

Two thirds to sell

We keep the other two thirds of the biochar to sell. Its quality has not been tested, and buyers have not been chosen.

Biochar is the char the retort produces. The facility does not exist yet. The city, the site, and who transports each material have not been decided.

04 / 11 · How it works

From food waste to char,in one system

Food waste goes in at one end, char comes out at the other. The color of the beads in the pipes shows what is flowing.

Flow summary: 35 kilograms of food waste per day (target) is sorted, mixed with water, and lifted into an unheated 5,000 liter digester. Bacteria break it down into biogas, and the leftover liquid (digestate) flows into an IBC container. The biogas is filtered and stored in a 2.5 cubic meter gas bag. From the bag, biogas lights and preheats the retort kiln at start-up, then only keeps a small pilot flame. Dry chips (dry biomass cut to 20 mm or smaller), 15 kilograms per day (target), from agribusinesses, enter through a hopper, and once hot, vapor from the chips is designed to keep it hot (not yet tested). Char leaves into a closed bin. Leftover gas can be sent to a burner or a flare at the facility (optional; subject to the safety review).

Terms
  • Digester: a closed tank where bacteria break food waste down into biogas.
  • Biogas: a flammable gas (mostly methane) made when material rots without air.
  • Retort: a kiln built as a closed tube that heats dry chips without air until they become char.
  • Digestate: the liquid and solids left over from the digester. Its quality as fertilizer has not been tested.
  • Char (also called biochar): what is left when chips are heated without air. Its quality has not been tested.
  • Dry chips: dry biomass cut to 20 mm or smaller (design assumption), from agribusinesses.

  • Digester. A 5,000 L HDPE tank, outdoors. Food waste becomes biogas here.
  • Gas module. A 2.5 m³ bag, a sulfur filter, and a pressure relief, under a netted canopy for natural ventilation. The net opening has not been tested.
  • Feed station. Sorting table, mixing tank, and pump. Food waste enters here every day.
  • Retort. A stainless steel tube with a screw conveyor. Dry chips move slowly, are heated without air, and leave as char.
  • Chimney. 4.34 m tall (design). Combustion exhaust leaves here, above the operator’s head.
  • Char bin. A closed bin with a water seal. Char is removed once cool, below 80 °C.
  • Operator panel. Start and stop buttons, green, orange, and red status lights, and an emergency stop. Safety does not depend on the screen.

07 / 11 · Safety

Safety designed infrom the start

Biogas is flammable. That is why the Lokalola design adds layered safeguards. All of it is still on paper and in the model.

Design features, not certification.

  1. 01

    Distance from the flame to the gas storage and treatment equipment: 5.62 m.

    Measured from the model, from the ignition source (combustion chamber, burner, chimney) to the nearest gas storage and treatment equipment. Design requirement: 5 m or more. Electrical panel to the same equipment: 5.57 m. The gas line is designed to be fully welded, with a valve near the burner. Hazardous-area zones will be set only after an expert hazard review.

  2. 02

    Emergency stop button (E-stop).

    Red, always visible. When pressed, all gas valves close and motors and pumps stop. It restarts only through a manual reset.

  3. 03

    Fire extinguisher.

    A portable fire extinguisher, visible from the operator’s walkway.

  4. 04

    Flame arrestor.

    A device that stops a flame from traveling back along the gas pipe. The design fits two.

  5. 05

    Vent.

    A pressure-relief pole taller than 3 m, uncovered. If gas pressure rises too high, gas is released upward, away from people.

  6. 06

    Hard-wired relays.

    Safety is wired from ordinary electrical relays, with no software. The screen and software only display information and never loosen a safeguard.

  7. 07

    Methane detector.

    Alarm at 20 percent of the lower flammable limit. At 40 percent, the gas valve closes automatically.

  8. 08

    Open-air installation.

    No building around the gas equipment. No gas pipes in closed channels, where gas could collect.

No safety review yet

There has been no hazard review by an expert and no hazardous-area zoning yet. The feed station and its pump are inside the 5 m line, and their zoning awaits that review. Safety on this page is design intent, not the result of an inspection.

Where we are now

The design is detailed.The machine does not exist yet.Prototype B1 comes first.

  • The design concept is set for the B1 test; the final retort design is fixed only after B1. The 3D model has had an internal review for layout.
  • Numbers are at the calculation and simulation level, not yet bench-tested or field-tested.
  • The request-for-quotation (RFQ) package for the B1 prototype is ready to send; nothing has been built.

Every design number on this page describes Lokalola Pilot. We are also studying a larger facility, Lokalola Hub, with a target of 500 kg wet waste and 250 kg dry biomass per operating day. That target is a study brief. Its design, cost, and capacity are unproven, and the B1 to B5 results will not carry over to it automatically.

  1. Next

    B1 Retort.

    Planned: the retort rig will be tested in an open area, with a substitute gas standing in for biogas. It answers four questions: can the retort run on its own heat, how much gas a cold start takes, whether tar stays under control, and whether the char is good quality. Estimated time once the B1 build is ordered: final design 1 week, fabrication 2 weeks, testing 2–3 weeks.

  2. B2 Feed pump.

    Will be tested with real food waste from food businesses for three weeks. Target: no clogging.

  3. B3 Safety components.

    Planned: quotes will be requested and the original certificates of the arrestor, gas detector, and solenoid valve will be checked.

  4. B4 Gas bag.

    Planned: a 2.5 m³ bag with a weight board under the canopy will be tested. Target pressure 5 mbar ± 1 mbar, including a wind test.

  5. B5 Pilot digester.

    Planned: a pilot digester at ambient air temperature will be run. Target: biogas 1.8 m³ per day or more, and recovery after a 14-day break.

B1 milestone
  1. Q1

    Can the retort run on its own heat?

    At 5 kg of chips per hour, the main flame is turned down to a small flame, and the tube wall stays above 420 °C for at least two hours.

  2. Q2

    How much gas does a cold start take?

    Target: 30 MJ or less, about 1.5 m³ of biogas. Current calculation: 32–38 MJ, so this target is a stretch.

  3. Q3

    Is tar under control?

    After five sessions of 30 kg each, the vapor holes are still at least 80 percent open, the motor does not jam, and no tar clumps in the hopper.

  4. Q4

    Is the char good quality?

    Tested in a laboratory. Target: volatile matter 25 percent or less, and fixed carbon 65 percent or more.

Summary; the full criteria are in the B1 test plan.

Backup plan, not a promise

If the results are poor: if Q1 narrowly fails, we raise the feed rate or add heat retention. If it fails by a wide margin, we fall back to a drum design, which would need about 45 kg of food waste per day instead of 35. That would raise the supply we need to secure.
